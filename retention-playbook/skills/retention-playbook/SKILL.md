# Retention Playbook

Use the supplied retention brief as the source of truth. This skill writes and checks strategy text only. It never sends, publishes, connects to an account, or promises a churn outcome.

## Required playbook areas

Create one play for each area:

1. Onboarding and activation: move a new customer to the activation event quickly, using a single clear next action, visible progress when appropriate, and a dismissible path.
2. Proactive retention: respond to a real risk signal such as falling usage, stalled setup, unresolved support, billing-page visits, or a renewal window.
3. Cancel flow: ask an exit-survey question, make a relevant allowed save offer, and always keep a clear, visible path to cancel. Do not hide, block, or trap cancellation.
4. Failed payment recovery: explain the payment issue, offer a direct update path, and use only a channel available in the brief.
5. Win-back: address a likely reason for leaving with a relevant reason to return and a measurable response metric.
6. Referral: ask after a genuine value moment, make sharing simple, and define the referral measurement.

Every play must state trigger, audience, a brief-approved channel, message, owner role, and success metric. A save offer can only be an offer allowed in the brief. Report missing or unsupported inputs as issues; do not silently invent them.

## Working guidance

- Prioritize time to value and the activation event rather than a feature tour.
- Prefer signals and audiences the team can actually observe and reach.
- Keep cancellation humane: one useful offer and a clear continue-cancelling route.
- Use reminders and recovery messages to help, not to make unsupported performance claims.
- Ask for referrals after activation, a milestone, renewal, or a notably good support moment.
- Never state guaranteed churn reductions or unsupported proof. Treat numeric advice not supplied by the brief as a plugin limit, not an upstream rule.
